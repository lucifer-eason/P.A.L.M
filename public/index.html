The product

From sketch to shoe

P.A.L.M started as a design for one athlete, Connor Palmer, who plays soccer, lifts, coaches and trains every day. His needs became the product's features.

Interchangeable outsoles

A flat herringbone indoor court sole for grip on gym floors, and a deep-lug outdoor sole for turf and concrete. Both come in the box.

Palmlock system

A midfoot lockdown strap that keeps the foot planted, adds stability and stops unwanted movement on cuts and lifts.

Ankle support

High padded ankle collar, reinforced heel counter and an ankle-supporting insole to reduce the risk of injury.

Carbon reinforcement

A carbon sidewall plate adds strength and lateral support for quick cuts and turns.

Cushion midsole

Shock-absorbing foam reduces impact on the joints and stays comfortable through long days on your feet.

Breathable mesh upper

A lightweight, flexible engineered mesh that keeps feet cool through daily training.

Launch colourways

Volt Ground

White and black upper on a yellow and grey sole. The hero colourway: bright, loud and made for the camera.

Step 1 · Target market

Who we're selling to

We segment by behaviour and lifestyle rather than age alone. Our target is the multi-surface athlete: active people aged 16 to 40 who train on more than one surface each week and are tired of carrying two or three pairs of shoes. Within that segment we focus on two personas.

GeographicUrban and suburban Canada, with a launch in major cities that have strong club-soccer and gym cultures
Demographic16 to 40 years old, students and young professionals, all genders, mid income
PsychographicPerformance-driven, values versatility and value for money, treats fitness as part of their identity
BehaviouralTrains 4+ times a week on 2+ surfaces, researches online before buying, loyal to brands that perform

01

Value-based pricing

P.A.L.M replaces two pairs worth about $300, so the customer sees $189.99 as a smart deal, even though it is priced above most single trainers. The price reflects the perceived value of versatility and injury protection.

02

Competition-based check

Premium trainers and turf shoes from major brands sell for $140 to $180. Pricing slightly above this range tells the customer P.A.L.M is a performance product, not a budget cross-trainer, while the bundle keeps it competitive.

03

Psychological pricing

$189.99 instead of $190 uses charm pricing, so the shoe is read as "$180-something" and stays under the $200 barrier that students like Jordan watch for.

04

Captive product pricing

Outsoles wear out before the upper. Selling replacement soles for $39.99 creates recurring revenue and extends the life of the shoe, which appeals to durability-focused buyers like Dana.

05

Discount pricing for segments

Quantity discounts for teams (15% on 10+ pairs) make coaches a sales channel. A student discount lowers the barrier for our primary persona without lowering the list price.

06

Cost-plus floor

Our price must also cover our costs. Estimated unit cost is about $58, landed. Selling at $189.99 direct, or about $95 wholesale to retailers, gives healthy margins at every level of the channel.

Where the $189.99 goes (retail channel)

$46Materials & manufacturing
$12Shipping & duties
$37P.A.L.M margin (marketing, R&D, profit)
$95Retailer markup

Wholesale price ≈ $95, with the retailer doubling to MSRP (keystone markup). On direct online sales, P.A.L.M keeps the retailer share, which funds launch marketing.

Customer

Channel A · Zero-level (direct)

palm-athletics.com

≈ 50% of sales

Our own online store with free returns, a sole-swap video guide and a size-fit quiz. Selling direct lets us keep higher margins, own customer data and have full control over the brand.

Why: Jordan researches and buys online and follows our social links straight to the store. It also makes subscribing to replacement outsoles easy.

Channel B · One-level (retailer)

Selected sports retailers

≈ 35% of sales

A limited number of specialty stores, such as national sporting-goods chains in big markets and independent soccer and running shops, with in-store displays where customers can try the outsole swap.

Why: Dana wants to try shoes on before buying. Selective distribution, rather than intensive distribution in discount stores, protects our premium image and price.

Channel C · Direct B2B

Teams, clubs & schools

≈ 15% of sales

A team-sales program with bulk pricing, custom colourways in club colours and fitting days at practices. We also run pop-up booths at tournaments and showcases.

Why: one coach like Dana can lead to 20+ pairs. Team buys create word of mouth among young players and their parents.

Where we won't sell, and why

Discount and big-box stores: price wars would weaken the premium positioning. Third-party marketplaces at launch: we lose control over price and the brand experience, and there's a risk of counterfeits. We'll reconsider after year one.
